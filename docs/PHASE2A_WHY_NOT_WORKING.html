Why Phase 2A Has Not Passed Yet

A simple explanation of the current results

Phase 2A is not failing because the code crashed. The simulator, PPO training, and fair comparison all work. The problem is that the agents do not show a steady benefit from pheromone across repeated runs.

Short answer: pheromone sometimes helps, but it sometimes does not help or is slightly worse than the same trained policy without pheromone. Because of this, we cannot honestly say that our learned agents reliably use pheromone yet.

Based on the completed original, simple-map, slow-fading, and scout-and-follow Phase 2A checks.
31 October 2026 · Development-only simulator results

1. What Phase 2A is checking

Our agents collect food and carry it back to a nest. They can see a small area around themselves. They may also see two pheromone maps: a food trail and a home trail.

Phase 2A asks one basic question:

Does a policy trained with pheromone do better than the same policy trained without pheromone?

To make this fair, we train two separate PPO models for every map and seed:

ModelWhat it seesWhy we use it
Full modelFood, nest, nearby agents, boundaries, its own carrying state, and pheromone.This is the normal model we want to study.
No-pheromone modelThe same information, except the pheromone values are hidden during both training and testing.This tells us whether pheromone itself is helping.
Random movementNo learned policy. It chooses actions randomly.This checks that PPO is learning something useful at all.

We use three training seeds: 7, 17, and 27. A seed changes the random starting point of learning and the order of experience. A good result must work across all three seeds, not only one lucky run.

2. What we tested

We first tested three normal maps. Then we tried two simpler maps with more food, so agents could make more repeat trips and have more chances to use trails. We also tested slower-fading pheromone and a scout-and-follow setup with one distant food cluster.

3. What the results say

For each seed, the important number is:

full model score − no-pheromone model score

Why this stops Phase 2A: every map has at least one seed where pheromone is not better. We need all three seeds to show a clear benefit before we say the effect is reliable.

The good news

  • Most full models did better than random movement: 23 out of 27 comparisons.
  • This means PPO is learning how to collect food. The project is not stuck because the basic task is impossible.
  • Pheromone helped in 13 out of 27 full-versus-no-pheromone comparisons.

The problem

  • Pheromone was tied or worse in 14 out of 27 comparisons.
  • Because the result changes with the seed, we cannot tell whether a good pheromone score is a real effect or just one good training run.

4. Why this is probably happening

We have not proved one single cause. These are the most likely reasons based on the experiments and the design of the task.

Likely reasonSimple explanationWhat the results support
The other local clues are already useful.Even without pheromone, agents can see nearby food, the nest, walls, nearby agents, and whether they are carrying food. They can learn a useful search-and-return rule from these clues.The no-pheromone models often deliver almost as much food as the full models.
The trail is local and fades, but that is not the whole problem.An agent sees only a small 3×3 area. We slowed fading from 10% to 2% per step, and food trails became easier to see.Food-trail visibility rose in every seed, but the full model still lost once. So longer-lasting trails alone do not solve the problem.
Making the deadline too tight destabilizes learning.The long scout-and-follow map was close: full PPO was faster on all seeds and tied once at the maximum score. We then trained the same task with a shorter deadline.In the short version, the full model delivered zero on two seeds. This is not a safe way to turn speed into a stronger score.
More training time did not fix the short version.We doubled the PPO budget for both full and no-pheromone policies, keeping every other setting the same.The full model still lost all three seeds. The short deadline is not just a small-training-budget problem.
Learning is unstable across seeds.PPO learns from random experience. One seed may find a useful behavior, while another seed learns a different behavior that ignores or misuses trails.The same map can be positive for one seed and negative for another.
The food positions change across maps.Food cells are sampled from the map seeds. A trail pattern learned on one set of maps may not help equally on another set.We see different outcomes across repeated training runs, even with the same map size.
The task may be solvable without trail-following.With eight agents searching at the same time, some agents may find food directly. The team can still score well even if nobody follows a trail.Full and no-pheromone models both usually beat random movement.
Important: these are good explanations to test next. They are not final proof. We should change one small thing at a time and run the same fair comparison again.

5. What this does not mean

  • It does not mean the simulator is broken.
  • It does not mean PPO cannot learn. It clearly beats random movement in nearly every run.
  • It does not prove that pheromone never helps.
  • It does not mean we should force an attack or detector dataset now.

It means we need one clean setup where the extra pheromone information helps in a repeatable way. Only then can we test whether a false pheromone attack damages that useful behavior.

6. What we should do next

Do not start Phase 2B yet. We have checked whether trails fade too fast, whether a clearer scout-and-follow setup helps, and whether extra training fixes the short version. The long version was close but did not pass; the short version stayed unstable. Before another experiment, the team must make one small new Phase 2A decision that answers one clear question.

  1. Use the saved local observations and action logs to check whether the full model reacts to food pheromone, or mostly follows food, nest, and wall clues.
  2. Choose one small clean-learning change only after that check, and write it down before coding it. The change must make following a food route genuinely useful, without giving agents global information.
  3. Keep the same fair full-versus-no-pheromone test, the same three seeds, and the same random check.
  4. Run every planned seed. Do not select a map because one run looks good.
  5. Start Phase 2B only if the full model is better than the no-pheromone model in all declared seeds.
Why we should wait: if we make a large attack dataset now, we may end up measuring attacks on a behavior that has not been shown to depend on pheromone. That would make the final conclusion weak.
